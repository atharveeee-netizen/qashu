Grover’s Algorithm

qc/algorithms

Solves unstructured search: given f:{0,1}n→{0,1} marking M “solution” strings out of N=2n total, find one - classically this needs O(N/M) oracle calls on average, Grover’s needs only O(N/M​). Key insight: this is a proven-optimal quadratic speedup, not a shortcut to exponential - no sequence of unitaries interleaved with oracle calls can beat O(N/M​) for a genuinely unstructured problem. Shor’s algorithm gets an exponential speedup instead, but only because factoring has extra algebraic structure (see The Quantum Algorithm Zoo) that Grover’s problem setup doesn’t have.

Oracle and phase kickback

The oracle acts as Uf​∣x,y⟩=∣x,y⊕f(x)⟩. Prepared against a target qubit in ∣−⟩, this becomes a pure phase flip on solutions - exactly the phase-kickback trick: Uf​∣x,−⟩=Pf​∣x⟩⊗∣−⟩ where Pf​∣x⟩=(−1)f(x)∣x⟩.

The geometric picture

Split the search space into ∣α⟩ (uniform superposition over the N−M non-solutions) and ∣β⟩ (uniform superposition over the M solutions). The uniform starting state is ∣ψ0​⟩=cosϑ0​∣α⟩+sinϑ0​∣β⟩ with sinϑ0​=M/N​ - a 2D problem living entirely in the {∣α⟩,∣β⟩} plane. Pf​ reflects the state across ∣α⟩; the diffusion operator D=H⊗n(2∣0⟩⟨0∣−1)H⊗n=2∣ψ0​⟩⟨ψ0​∣−1 reflects it back across ∣ψ0​⟩. Two reflections compose into a rotation: one Grover iteration G=DPf​ rotates the state by 2ϑ0​ toward ∣β⟩.

After m iterations, ∣ψm​⟩=cosϑm​∣α⟩+sinϑm​∣β⟩ with ϑm​=(1+2m)ϑ0​, and success probability sin2ϑm​. Since this is a rotation, overshooting past ϑm​≈π/2 swings the probability back down - there’s a sharp optimum, not a monotonic improvement:

m≈4π​MN​​
